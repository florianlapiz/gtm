Opening

The Core Thesis

Most outbound campaigns don't fail because of bad tools or poor deliverability. They fail because the sender doesn't truly understand the customer's problem. The fix is sequence, not volume.

Great outbound results don't come from more emails or better tools. They come from three things, in this exact order:

  1. Deep problem understanding — the four layers of customer pain, the workaround test, the value proposition that holds up under pressure.
  2. Relevant messaging — emails that could only have been sent to that one person, built on signals the recipient knows are real.
  3. Targeted automation — a lean stack that scales research and personalization, not spam.

In that sequence. Skip the first step and the rest is wasted effort. This manual walks each layer with frameworks, examples, and the operator moves you can run this week.

The Sequence

Problem → Relevance → Automation. Reverse the order and you get a tool stack that produces spam at scale. Most founders get this wrong.

Part 1 · Block A

Problem Space Before Outbound

Before writing a single outbound email, you need to understand your customer's problem across four layers. Most campaigns fail here — not at the tool level.

1.1The Four Layers of Customer Pain

Adapted from Maja Voje's GTM Strategist framework. Every real B2B pain sits across all four layers — and effective outbound names at least two of them in the first two sentences.

LayerWhat it asksExample — HR-Tech SaaS
FunctionalWhat's broken, slow, manual?"We track applicants in Excel, candidates fall through the cracks."
EmotionalWhat frustrates or scares them?"I'm worried we're losing top talent to faster competitors."
SocialHow do they look to boss/board/peers?"My CEO asks every week why positions aren't filled."
FinancialWhere's money being burned?"Each open role costs us €3k/month in lost productivity."

An email that only hits the functional layer ("we automate your recruiting") gets ignored. An email that hits emotional + financial ("you're losing €9k/month on 3 open roles, and top candidates go to competitors because your process is too slow") gets replies.

1.2The Workaround Test

Ask one question: "What's the worst workaround your target customer uses today?"

If the answer is "Excel spreadsheet", "manually by email", "nothing", or "an intern handles it" — you've found a real problem. The worse the current workaround, the higher the willingness to pay. If there's no workaround at all, the problem doesn't exist.

1.3The Value Proposition Bridge

Before outbound makes sense, this bridge has to hold:

Customer Profile
What they live with

Jobs: What are they trying to accomplish?
Pains: What makes those jobs hard?
Gains: What outcome would delight them?

Your Value Map
What you offer

Products: What do you sell?
Pain Relievers: How do you reduce specific pains?
Gain Creators: How do you create specific gains?

If you can't name a specific customer pain for each pain reliever — you lack clarity. No tool can fix that.

1.4The Three-Sentence Test

Can you answer each of these in one sentence?

  • Who exactly is your customer? (Not "all B2B companies")
  • What problem do you solve? (In customer language, not feature language)
  • Why you vs. alternatives? (Including the alternative of doing nothing)

If you can't, research is your next step, not campaigns.

Part 1 · Block B

Relevance Over Personalization

Most outbound guides say "personalize your emails." That advice produces emails like "I saw your LinkedIn post." Crawford's counter-thesis: relevance beats personalization. Every time.

2.1The Difference in Practice

Personalized · weak

"Hi Thomas, I noticed you work at Company X and your LinkedIn post about AI was really interesting. We help SaaS companies with lead generation…"

The recipient thinks: they Googled my name. So what.

Relevant · strong

"Thomas, you have 3 open SDR roles on your careers page. That means your outbound needs to scale — but new SDRs take 3–6 months to ramp. What if you could get the pipeline output of a 3-person SDR team in 2 weeks, without hiring anyone?"

The second email proves you researched the company and understand what's happening right now. You're talking about their problem, not your product.

2.2Micro-Campaigns — Quality Over Volume

Crawford's rule: 50 deeply researched accounts beat 5,000 generic contacts.

  • 5,000-contact campaigns force generic messaging ("one size fits all")
  • 50-contact campaigns allow account-specific research
  • Reply rate for a 50-account micro-campaign: typically 8–15%
  • Reply rate for a 5,000-contact spray-and-pray: 1–2%
  • 50 × 12% = 6 real conversations. That's all a seed-stage startup needs.

2.3Creative Data Sources

This is where Clay (or Exa, Apollo, your equivalent) becomes a lever. Not as an email finder, but as a research engine.

Data SourceWhat It RevealsOutbound Angle
Job PostingsWho's hiring = has a problem + budget"You're hiring a Head of Sales — sales is top priority right now."
Tech StackWhich tools they already use"You use HubSpot but no outbound tool — does that mean inbound alone isn't enough anymore?"
Website ScrapingPositioning, messaging, target audience"Your website targets Enterprise, but your team has 12 people — how do you scale sales?"
Funding DataJust raised = budget + pressure to grow"Congrats on the Series A — the clock is ticking. What's the pipeline plan for Q3?"
G2 / Capterra ReviewsWhat customers say about competitors"The #1 complaint about [competitor] is X — that's exactly what we do differently."

2.4AI Research Agent — The Force Multiplier

The most powerful pattern for micro-campaigns: let an AI agent run a mini-analysis per account.

  • Input: Website, job postings, company data, news
  • Prompt: "What's the most likely problem this company has in the area of [your topic]? Formulate a hypothesis in 2 sentences."
  • Output: An account-specific hypothesis you embed directly in the email

This is where automation and relevance converge. You're no longer writing generically, but you're also not spending 30 minutes per account on manual research.

Part 1 · Block C

Pain-First Copywriting

Most cold emails follow this pattern: "We are Company X. We do Y. Our features are Z. Want to chat?" That's a pitch. Nobody replies to pitches from strangers. The Pain-First framework reverses it.

3.1The Four-Sentence Framework

Name the Problem

Show you understand what's happening at their company. Use real data.
"You have 3 open SDR roles that have been posted for 6 weeks."

Show the Implication

What does this problem mean for them? Make the pain concrete.
"That means your pipeline isn't growing, while your burn rate keeps running."

Hint at the Solution

No feature list. Only the outcome.
"We helped [similar company] build the pipeline of a 3-person SDR team in 3 weeks — without hiring anyone."

Soft CTA

Not "can we jump on a call?" — too much commitment.
"Would it make sense for me to show you how that looks?"

The Constraint

Total email: 4 sentences. Under 80 words. No attachments. No HTML. No tracking pixels. No images.

3.2Three Copywriting Rules for Founders

  1. Write like a human, not a marketer. No buzzwords, no corporate-speak. Imagine you're writing a text to an acquaintance who happens to fit your ICP.
  2. One idea per email. Don't cram three value props into one message. One hypothesis, one pain, one question.
  3. Subject lines: short, lowercase, specific. "quick question about your sales team" beats "Revolutionize Your Sales Pipeline with AI-Powered Automation" every single time.

3.3A Working Example, End-to-End

Here's how the framework comes together on a real send:

From: florian@yourcompany.io
To: thomas@targetcompany.com
Subject: 3 open SDR roles → 6 weeks
Thomas,

Noticed you've had 3 SDR roles posted for ~6 weeks.

At your stage that usually means pipeline coverage is slipping while burn keeps running — and new hires need 3–6 months to ramp anyway.

We helped Hive get the output of a 3-person SDR team in 3 weeks, without hiring anyone.

Worth a 10-min look to see if it'd map to your setup?

— Florian

Five sentences, 73 words. Plain text. One hypothesis, one number, one soft CTA. Could only be sent to Thomas — not to 500 other people.

Part 1 · Block D

Minimal Viable Outbound Stack

A €1k/month HubSpot Enterprise setup at Pre-Seed is a slow death. So is a Clay subscription before 200 accounts/month. The right stack at the right phase is leverage. The wrong one is friction with a price tag.

4.1The Seed-Stage Stack

Clay / Exa — Research & Enrichment
↓
Instantly — Email Sending & Warm-up
↓
Claude / GPT — Personalization & Copy
↓
Attio / Folk / Notion — Lightweight Tracking
↓
Cal.com / Calendly — Scheduling

That's it. No Salesloft, no Outreach, no 8-tool stack. Total monthly cost: €200–300.

4.2Technical Basics You Can't Skip

  • Burner domains. Never send from your main domain. A blocked domain = all your email traffic compromised. Buy 2–3 similar look-alike domains.
  • Warm-up. New domains need 2–3 weeks of warm-up before you can send real outreach. Instantly handles this automatically. Don't skip it.
  • Volume. Start with max 25–30 emails per day per mailbox. Not 200 on day one — that's the fastest way to land in spam permanently.
  • Verification. Verify every email address before sending. Bounce rate above 3% = domain reputation destroyed.

4.3The Mission-Critical Filter

For every new tool, every new tactic, every new channel — ask three questions:

  1. Does this directly move me toward my next milestone?
  2. Is there evidence this works in my specific market?
  3. Can I execute this well with my current resources?

If not 3 of 3, it's a distraction, not a priority.

Distraction Hit List for Seed-Stage Founders

Redesigning the logo again. Perfecting the website before talking to customers. Building features nobody asked for. Attending conferences without a plan. Evaluating a 3rd tool instead of starting with the 1st.

Part 2

The Live Build Method

How to take one real target account from blank page to relevant cold email in under 15 minutes. The method we ran live in the workshop, now broken down step by step.

Problem Check (2 min)

Describe your best customer in 2 sentences. What's their biggest problem — in their words, not yours? What do they do today instead? (Workaround Test.) If you can't answer clearly, the tools won't save you. Stop and do customer interviews first.

Account Research (3 min)

Enrich the target company's website. Pull job postings — what are they hiring for? What does that reveal about priorities? Check the tech stack. Run the AI agent: "What's the most likely problem this company has, based on this data?"

Draft the Email (3 min)

Apply the Pain-First framework. Problem → Implication → Solution → Soft CTA. Goal: an email that could only be sent to this one company. Then craft the subject line.

The Crawford Check (2 min)

Read your draft. Ask: "Could this email be sent to 500 other companies?" If yes, it's not good enough. Rewrite the first sentence until it names something only that one company could recognize as theirs.

Operator Q&A

The Hard Questions

The four questions that come up after every workshop. Each one with a 90-second answer and the anti-rule that goes with it.

Q1What does the actual Founder-GTM stack cost?

Answer: €200–250/month, six layers, AI-native primitives over legacy SaaS.

  • CRM: Attio
  • Sequencing: Instantly
  • Research + Workflows: Claude Code
  • Signal Discovery: Exa.ai
  • Meeting Capture: Fireflies + Plaud
  • Scheduling: Cal.com

Anti-rule: Don't set up HubSpot Enterprise at Seed. Don't use Clay below 200 accounts/month. The stack optimizes for leverage per hour, not scale.

Q2What about AI SDRs — do they replace this?

Short answer: Not yet, and maybe not soon.

  • What AI SDRs do well: high-volume, low-personalization, top-of-funnel.
  • What they don't do well yet: signal-stacking, hyper-relevance, multi-threading. The quality is still below what a human with good tooling produces.
  • The bet: AI SDRs replace the bottom 30% of outbound work (volume scoring, basic personalization) in 2026. The top 70% — the signal-driven, champion-hypothesis, multi-threading work — stays human-driven with AI augmentation for another 2–3 years.

Anti-rule: Don't automate sales. Automate the research, qualification, and signal-monitoring that feeds sales. Sales itself is still a human game at your stage.

Q3How do I sell Enterprise at Seed without case studies?

Reframe: At Seed, Enterprise isn't a sales motion. It's a design-partnership motion that becomes sales in Month 7.

Phase 1 · Months 1–3

Sign 3 design partners at 50% off, 12-month commitment. R&D with revenue. Pitch: "Build this with us. Shape the roadmap. 50% off for 12 months. You get influence, we get proof."

Phase 2 · Months 4–6

Deploy, document objections, turn into case studies and security docs. Make the asks you couldn't make in Phase 1.

Phase 3 · Month 7+

Now run systematic Enterprise outbound with assets in hand. Pricing tiers 3–5x SMB. SOC2 (Type I min, Type II roadmap), SSO, DPA, data residency story.

Anti-rule: Running Enterprise sales in Phase 1 without case studies or security docs. Deals die at procurement.

Q4When do I hire my first GTM person?

Rule: Hire for the motion you've already proven, not the one you want to scale into.

StageARRFirst Hire
0–10 customers€0–500kFounder only. No agencies. Learn personally.
10–30 customers€500k–1.5MSDR (Business Buyer) or Content Marketer (Technical Buyer)
30–80 customers€1.5M–4MFirst AE + full-time SDR. Founder steps out of <€25k deals.
80+ customers€4M+Head of Sales (NOT VP), Marketing Manager FTE, 2–3 SDRs, first CS.

Anti-rule: Hiring a VP Sales before €3–5M ARR. VPs are multipliers, not pioneers. Hire a senior AE with hunter mentality instead.

Walkaway

Five Takeaways

The whole manual compresses into these five rules. Each one actionable this week.

Rule 01
Problem First, Tool Second

Understand your customer's problem across all four layers before you touch a tool. If you can't describe their pain in their words, no outbound will work.

Rule 02
Relevance > Personalization

Show you understand their business, not that you can Google their name. Write emails that could only be sent to that one person.

Rule 03
50 Right > 5,000 Wrong

Micro-campaigns with deep research beat spray-and-pray every time. You need 5–10 real conversations, not 75 unsubscribe replies.

Rule 04
Pain-First Copywriting

Problem → Implication → Solution → Soft CTA. Four sentences, under 80 words. Plain text. One idea per email.

Rule 05 · Priority
Mission-Critical Focus

One ICP, one channel, one campaign. Measure. Learn. Then scale. Not 5 channels at once. Not 5 segments at once. Not 5 messages at once.
